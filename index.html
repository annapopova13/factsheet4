Equality Monitoring in the European Research Area

From diverse national reporting to a lasting policy framework.

Gender equality has been an ERA priority since 2012, but policy without monitoring limits the ability to track impact and make evidence-based decisions. The 2025–2027 ERA Policy Agenda shifts the focus from adopting policies to implementing, monitoring and measuring their impact — expanding into intersectionality, mainstreaming across other ERA actions, and gender budgeting.

Why equality monitoring matters

Data and monitoring are essential for evidence-based policymaking, impact tracking and continuous policy feedback. At the moment, monitoring in the ERA is covered only partially, or on an ad-hoc basis — spread across the triannual She Figures report, ERA monitoring reports and tools, and outcomes from Horizon Europe projects such as UniSAFE, GENDERACTIONplus, GenderSAFE and INSPIRE.

Since 2012, the European Institute for Gender Equality (EIGE) has run EU-wide comparable monitoring of institutional mechanisms for gender equality — tracking the implementation of the Beijing Platform for Action and the EU Gender-Based Violence Directive. It's a working framework the ERA can draw inspiration from. Visit EIGE →

Building blocks of a monitoring system

Whatever shape a national system takes, four components keep coming up as the difference between a policy on paper and one that's actually tracked.

01

Political ownership & accountability

A clear mandate, a responsible body, and accountability for results are a must.

02

Human resources

Dedicated staff at both national and EU level are necessary to keep monitoring running.

03

Operational system

Coordination structures, tools, and consultation and validation mechanisms need to be in place.

04

Gender statistics & communication

Regular data production and effective dissemination are a core part of any monitoring framework.
